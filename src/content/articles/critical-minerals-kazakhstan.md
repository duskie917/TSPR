Kazakhstan is already the world’s largest producer of uranium. It also holds significant deposits of other metals in demand for batteries, electronics and clean-energy technology. As the EU, the United States, China and others race to secure supply chains, those resources have become a diplomatic asset.

## From extraction to processing

The central question is whether Kazakhstan can move beyond exporting raw ore. Processing and refining capture more value and create skilled jobs — but they require energy, water, capital and environmental safeguards.

## Competing suitors

Memoranda on critical raw materials have been signed with multiple partners. That competition is useful. But memoranda are not mines; transparent licensing, stable tax rules and credible environmental and community standards will determine which projects reach production.

## Getting governance right

Resource booms have a mixed record. The lessons from Kazakhstan’s own oil sector — on local content, revenue management and transparency — apply here. If they are learned, critical minerals could help diversify the economy rather than deepen its dependence on commodities.
