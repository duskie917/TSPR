Turkmenistan holds some of the largest natural-gas reserves on the planet, yet its export options remain narrow. Since the late 2000s, China has become by far its largest customer via the Central Asia–China pipeline system.

## Projects long on promise

Two alternatives have been discussed for years: the **TAPI pipeline** to Afghanistan, Pakistan and India, and a **Trans-Caspian pipeline** to Azerbaijan and onward to Europe. Both face security, financing, legal and political hurdles.

## Neutrality as constraint

Turkmenistan’s policy of permanent neutrality has shielded it from some entanglements but has also limited its participation in regional and multilateral frameworks that could help finance and secure new routes.

## What could change

Higher European interest in non-Russian gas, improving regional relations and new interconnections in the Caspian could make incremental options — small-scale swaps, LNG or compressed gas across the sea — more viable than mega-pipelines. Diversification may come in small steps rather than one grand project.
