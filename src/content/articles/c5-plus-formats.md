A decade ago, Central Asia was usually discussed as a set of bilateral relationships. Today, the region is increasingly engaged as a bloc. The **“C5+” format** — the five Central Asian states plus one external partner — has multiplied, with summits involving the United States, the European Union, China, Russia, Japan, South Korea, India, Germany and others.

## Why external partners like the format

For outside powers, meeting all five leaders at once is efficient and signals respect for the region’s agency. It also allows them to discuss genuinely regional issues: transport corridors, water, energy grids and critical minerals.

## Why Central Asian capitals like it

For the region, the format amplifies bargaining power. Five states negotiating together are more attractive partners than any one alone — and multiple C5+ formats competing for attention reinforce a multi-vector foreign policy.

## The catch

The format’s value depends on the five actually coordinating. Differences in size, economic model and political system remain significant. Regular Consultative Meetings of Central Asian heads of state have helped, but the region still lacks permanent joint institutions.

If C5+ remains a series of photo opportunities, its novelty will fade. If it becomes a channel for joint projects, it could be one of the most consequential diplomatic developments in the region since independence.
