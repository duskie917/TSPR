For decades, Uzbekistan was a natural-gas exporter. Today, a growing population, expanding industry and an inefficient, gas-heavy power sector mean the country increasingly needs to import gas — particularly in winter.

## A structural, not seasonal, problem

Winter shortages make headlines, but the underlying problem is structural. Domestic consumption has grown faster than production, and much of it is used inefficiently: in old power plants, leaky distribution networks and buildings with little insulation.

## The reform agenda

Tashkent has responded with an ambitious programme:

- **Tariff reform**, moving energy prices gradually toward cost recovery while protecting vulnerable households.
- **Independent power producers** and large solar and wind projects procured through competitive auctions.
- **Modernising thermal plants** so each unit of gas produces more electricity.

## Risks to manage

Tariff increases are politically sensitive. Renewable projects require grid investment and balancing capacity. And import dependence creates new geopolitical exposure.

The direction is right. The challenge is sequencing — ensuring that efficiency gains and new supply arrive before the next cold winter tests public patience.
